Module 01 · Foundation

What Is a Business Cycle & Why It Drives Currencies

Before you read a single indicator, you must understand what you are actually looking at and why business cycle divergence generates the largest, most sustained currency moves in the entire market.

Mentor

A business cycle is the economy's natural rhythm of expansion and contraction. It is not random. It has identifiable phases, identifiable signals, and identifiable turning points. Every major multi-month currency move in history was driven by a business cycle divergence. The 2022 USD surge (Fed hiking into EZ stagflation), the 2013 JPY collapse (BoJ regime change), the 2020 risk-off and subsequent AUD recovery — all of it traces back to cycle analysis. This is not one tool among many. It is the engine that powers every sustained trade.

The Core Mechanism

Business cycle → Central bank response → Interest rate change → Real yield differential → Capital flows → Currency direction. The cycle is Step 1. Everything else is downstream of it. When two economies are at different phases, their CBs set rates at different levels and in different directions. Capital flows from the low-yield, late-cycle economy into the high-yield, expanding economy. That capital flow IS the currency move.

Why Leading Indicators Give You the Edge

Leading indicators move 3–12 months before the economy turns. CBs react to lagging data. The financial press reacts to coincident data. You read leading data. This means you see the cycle turning before the CB acts on it, before headlines confirm it, and before the crowd positions for it. That structural time advantage is the edge Layer 1 gives you over everyone who reads only the GDP headline.

Module 04 · The 20 Indicators

The 7 Leading Indicators — Your Primary Trading Signals

Leading indicators move 3–12 months before the economy. These tell you where the cycle is going. You position on leading indicators. Coincident indicators merely confirm what you already knew. The crowd reacts to coincident data — you were already in.

Mentor

The most important rule about leading indicators: direction beats level, always. A PMI of 49.2 rising from 43 is more bullish than a PMI of 55 falling from 60. The direction tells you where the economy is going. The level tells you where it is. You are trading the future, not the present. Read the direction first. Every time.

PMI
PMI Composite & New Orders Sub-index
Leading · 1–3 Month Lead · Priority #1
Measures
Business survey diffusion index. Above 50 = expansion. Below 50 = contraction. The New Orders sub-index is the most forward-looking component of any PMI — it shows future production demand before it appears anywhere else in the data.
Thresholds
50 = crossover. Above 55 = strong expansion. Below 45 = meaningful contraction. A 49 rising from 44 beats a 54 falling from 60.
Reading rule
Require 2–3 consecutive months before calling a trend. Always check New Orders vs. Headline: if New Orders is below 50 while headline is above 50, the headline will follow lower within 2–3 months.
CB implication
PMI below 50 for 3 months = CB will discuss rate cuts within 2 meetings. PMI above 55 for 3 months = CB thinking about hiking pace.
TradingEconomics.com (all countries) · FRED:ISM_MAN_PMI (US)Released: 1st business day each month
YC
Yield Curve (2yr/10yr Spread)
Leading · 12–18 Month Lead · Priority #2
Measures
The 2yr minus 10yr government yield spread. The bond market's collective forecast of future conditions and CB rate path. Inversion = bond market pricing recession and aggressive cuts within 12–18 months. Has predicted every US recession since 1970.
Thresholds
Steep +1.5%+ = healthy expansion. Flat 0–+0.5% = late cycle. Inverted (negative) = recession signal. How it uninverts matters: bull steepening (short end falling) = Fed cutting aggressively = bear USD.
Build it
Type TVC:US02Y-TVC:US10Y directly into TradingView search bar for a live spread chart. Repeat for any country using country code prefix.
TVC:US02Y−TVC:US10Y · TVC:DE02Y−TVC:DE10Y · TVC:GB02Y−TVC:GB10YLive on TradingView
CS
HY & IG Credit Spreads
Leading · 3–6 Month Lead · Earliest Financial Stress Signal
Measures
Yield premium corporate bonds pay over government bonds. Compensates investors for default risk. Rising spreads = financial system pricing increased corporate default probability. Real money, real risk — not a survey.
Thresholds
HY below 350bp = healthy. 350–500bp = stress building. 500–700bp = contraction signal. Above 800bp = crisis. 2008 peak: 1,800bp. COVID peak: 1,100bp.
Why it leads
Wider borrowing costs → companies cut capex and hiring immediately → employment falls in 2–4 months → GDP falls in 3–6 months. Credit moves faster than any government data release.
FRED:BAMLH0A0HYM2 (US HY) · FRED:BAMLC0A0CM (US IG) · FRED:BAMLHE00EHYIOAS (EZ HY)Free via TradingView FRED prefix
CC
Consumer Confidence Index
Leading · 3–6 Month Lead on Spending
Measures
Survey of household expectations about personal finances and economy. Changes in sentiment lead actual consumer spending by 3–6 months. Consumer spending = 65–70% of GDP in most developed economies.
Thresholds
Rate of change more important than level. A 35-point fall in 6 months = spending contraction signal regardless of absolute level. Conference Board (US): above 100 = optimistic. GfK (UK/EZ): negative readings are common, watch direction.
Lead-lag
Confidence falls → Retail sales falls 2–3 months later → GDP falls 3–6 months later. You have a 6-month GDP forecast the moment confidence data drops.
FRED:UMCSENT (UMich) · FRED:CONCCONF (Conference Board) · TradingEconomics (global)Monthly
BP
Building Permits & Housing Starts
Leading · 6–18 Month Lead on GDP
Measures
Construction permits issued and housing starts. Mortgage-rate sensitive — responds immediately to rate hike impact. Each permit represents 6–18 months of future employment, materials demand, and income. A 12–18 month GDP forecast in a single number.
Cascade
Rate hike → Mortgage rates rise → Permits fall (Month 2–3) → Starts fall (Month 4–6) → Construction jobs fall (Month 7–9) → Materials demand fall (Month 10–12) → GDP falls (Month 15–18).
Signal threshold
A 20%+ decline from peak over 6 months = significant GDP headwind in 9–18 months. The 2006 US permits collapse (−30% YoY) predicted the 2008 crisis two years in advance.
FRED:PERMIT (US Building Permits) · FRED:HOUST (Housing Starts)Released: ~3rd week each month
M2
Money Supply Growth (M2)
Leading · 6–18 Month Lead on Inflation
Measures
Total money supply: cash, demand deposits, savings, money market funds. Rapid M2 growth = future inflation and activity surge. The 2021 US M2 surge (+27% YoY) predicted the 2022 inflation crisis 12–18 months ahead.
Threshold
Above 10% YoY sustained for 6+ months = inflation risk building materially. M2 contracting (rare) = deflationary pressure, CB likely behind the curve on cuts. Most useful at extremes.
Currency
Rapid M2 expansion = currency debasement = bearish in real terms despite possible nominal rate support. Especially critical during QE programs which expand M2 rapidly.
FRED:M2SL (US M2) · ECB SDW for EZ M3Released monthly
EQ
Equity Market & Breadth
Leading · 3–9 Month Lead · Use with Caution
Measures
Equity markets price economic conditions 6–9 months ahead. Sustained bear market (−20%+) historically precedes recessions. Breadth (% stocks above 200-day MA, advance-decline line) more reliable than headline index alone.
Be cautious
Markets have predicted 9 of the last 5 recessions. Equities can fall 20% for non-economic reasons. Always confirm equity signals with PMI and credit spreads before treating as economic signals.
Sector rotation
Defensives (utilities, staples, healthcare) outperforming cyclicals (discretionary, tech, materials) = institutional money repositioning for slowdown even before the index falls.
TradingView: SPX, VIX, sector ETFs · Stockcharts.com for breadth dataReal-time

The 6 Coincident Indicators — Confirmation Layer

Coincident indicators tell you where the economy is right now. They confirm your leading read was correct. You already positioned on leading indicators. The coincident data gives you conviction to hold and justifies your thesis to any doubter.

GDP
Gross Domestic Product
Coincident · Most Watched · Quarterly
Measures
Total output: C (Consumer) + I (Investment) + G (Government) + NX (Net Exports). Always read composition, not just the headline. Who drove the growth tells you if it is sustainable.
Composition rules
GDP beat driven by inventory build = fade it (next quarter will drag). Beat driven by consumer + investment = sustain. Miss where consumer spending held = less bad than the headline implies.
Rate of change
−0.2%, −0.9%, −1.4% (worsening) = stay short aggressively. −1.4%, −0.9%, −0.2% (decelerating losses) = begin covering shorts. The second-order trend is the most actionable signal.
BEA.gov (US) · Eurostat (EZ) · TradingEconomics.com (all countries)Quarterly: Advance, Preliminary, Final
NFP
Non-Farm Payrolls & Employment
Coincident · Highest Single-Event Market Impact
Measures
Jobs added or lost excluding agricultural. US NFP = single highest market-impact data release in global macro. Major miss or beat = 100–200 pip moves in minutes.
How to read
Use 3-month moving average. Single months are routinely revised by 50–100K+. Three consecutive misses > any single print. Always check participation rate and hours worked alongside the headline job count.
Cycle signal
200K+/month consistently = mid expansion. 100–200K = slowing. Below 100K = late cycle. Negative = recession confirmed. Compare vs. expectation — the surprise direction matters as much as the absolute number.
FRED:PAYEMS · BLS.gov for full detail1st Friday each month, 8:30 AM EST
IP
Industrial Production & Capacity Utilisation
Coincident · Key for Manufacturing Economies
Measures
Output of factories, mines, and utilities. Critical for Germany, Japan, South Korea where IP is a direct GDP proxy. Germany's industrial production is practically a leading indicator for EZ-wide GDP.
Reading rule
9+ consecutive months of declining IP with worsening rate = structural manufacturing recession. YoY above +3% = robust. YoY negative for 6+ months = recession confirmed.
Capacity utilisation
Above 80% = near-maximum capacity = inflationary = CB will hike. Below 75% = significant slack = CB can hold or cut. Released alongside IP most economies.
FRED:INDPRO (US) · FRED:CAPUTLB50001SQ (US Capacity Util) · Eurostat for EZ~15th of each month
RS
Retail Sales
Coincident · Consumer Demand Proxy
Measures
Monthly change in retail store sales. Consumer spending = 65–70% of GDP, making this one of the most direct GDP proxies available. Use core (ex-autos, ex-gas) to remove volatile categories.
Lead-lag
Retail sales lags consumer confidence by 2–3 months. You got the signal from confidence weeks ago. This either confirms (hold) or contradicts (reassess). Rare for it to contradict a sustained confidence signal.
Revisions
Three consecutive downward revisions = the data is softer than initial prints suggest, even if each revised number is still positive. Watch revision direction across 3 months.
FRED:RSAFS (Advance Retail Sales, US)~2nd week of each month
PCE
Personal Income, Spending & PCE Deflator
Coincident · Fed's Preferred Inflation Gauge
Measures
Personal income = what households earn. Personal spending = what they spend. PCE deflator = Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Core PCE 2% is the Fed's explicit target. Most important single release for Fed policy forecasting.
PCE rule
Core PCE above 2% and rising = hawkish. At 2% stable = neutral. Falling below 2% = CB discusses cuts. Core PCE above 3.5% = Fed must hike more than priced = USD bullish surprise risk.
Income vs spending
Income rising but spending falling = households saving, cautious = bearish growth outlook. Income flat but spending rising = savings drawdown = unsustainable consumption boom.
FRED:PCE · FRED:DPCCRG3M086SBEA (Core PCE YoY)Last business day each month
TB
Trade Balance & Current Account
Coincident · Structural Currency Pressure
Measures
Difference between exports and imports (trade balance) and the broader flow including investment income (current account). Widening deficit = country spending more than it earns abroad = structural demand for foreign currency = structural headwind.
Cycle connection
Mid expansion = imports surge (consumers buying foreign goods) = trade deficit widens. Contraction = imports fall = deficit narrows. A widening deficit AND currency above PPP = doubly vulnerable to reversion.
Commodity FX
For AUD, CAD, NOK: current account is commodity-price driven. China PMI + iron ore moves AUD's current account more than RBA decisions on many months.
FRED:BOPGSTB (US Trade Balance) · TradingEconomics for all countries~5 weeks after month end

The 7 Lagging Indicators — What CBs React To

Lagging indicators confirm a trend already underway. They are what CBs use to justify decisions — reacting to what has already happened. Understanding the lag explains why CBs always seem behind the curve, and why that gap is your structural edge.

Mentor — Critical Warning

The most expensive mistake in macro trading is using lagging indicators to make entry decisions. Unemployment at 3.9% does not mean the economy is healthy right now. It means it was healthy 3–6 months ago. By the time unemployment rises visibly, the currency has already fallen substantially. Lagging indicators explain the past. Leading indicators predict the future. Trade the future.

Module 07 · Skill Building

The 8 Trend-Reading Rules — Extract Signal from Noise

Reading one indicator print means nothing. Reading a trend correctly generates tradeable signals. These 8 rules govern how you extract a clean directional verdict from noisy, revised, seasonally adjusted economic data.

01

Direction beats level — always

A PMI of 49.2 rising from 43 over 5 months is more bullish than a PMI of 55 falling from 60. Direction tells you where the economy is going. Level tells you where it is. You trade the future, not the present. Every indicator must be assessed for direction before level. No exceptions.

02

Require 2–3 consecutive prints before calling a trend

A single data point is not a trend. One weak NFP could be a strike, snowstorm, or calendar quirk. One hot CPI could be a base effect. Two consecutive prints in the same direction = a developing trend. Three = a confirmed trend. This single rule saves you from dozens of head-fake entries every year.

03

Smooth volatile series with 3–6 month moving averages

NFP, retail sales, and industrial production have enormous month-to-month volatility from seasonal quirks, weather, and sampling noise. Plot a 3-month moving average over any volatile monthly series before drawing conclusions. Prints of +280K, −40K, +190K look chaotic raw; the 3-month average shows a clean deceleration from +210K to +143K — that is the real signal. Chart it. Do not read a table.

04

Watch the rate of change OF the rate of change

This is the single highest-value trend-reading skill. −0.2%, −0.9%, −1.4% (worsening) is a completely different trade from −1.4%, −0.9%, −0.2% (decelerating losses = trough forming), even though both show three negative GDP prints. The first says stay short aggressively. The second says start covering and building longs. Always ask: is the deterioration or improvement itself accelerating or decelerating?

05

Use YoY for big picture, recent months annualised for the turn

Year-over-year smooths seasonal distortions. Use it for big-picture trend assessment. But if inflation was high 12 months ago, this year's YoY will look artificially low (base effect). Always check the last 3 months annualised alongside YoY. If YoY says inflation is falling but recent months annualised show reacceleration, the YoY headline will catch up — and you are seeing it first.

06

Confirm with breadth — multiple indicators together

One indicator turning is a clue. Three to five indicators turning together in the same direction at the same time is a verdict. PMI alone falling = monitor only. PMI + confidence + building permits + credit spreads all turning negative = high-conviction contraction thesis. The more independent signals aligning, the higher the conviction and the larger the appropriate position size.

07

Watch whether revisions confirm or fight the move

Most economic releases are revised in subsequent months. Consistent revision direction is information. Three consecutive NFP prints revised down (e.g. 180K→140K, 165K→120K, 190K→145K) tells you the labour market is softer than initial prints showed — even if each revised number is still positive. Revisions consistently moving in one direction = the data is quietly confirming a trend the headlines obscured.

08

Chart 12–24 months visually rather than reading a table

The eye catches slope changes and inflection points in 2 seconds that take minutes to see in a column of numbers. Whenever assessing any indicator for a trend, plot the last 12–24 months before drawing any conclusion. This is how you catch the decelerating deterioration (trough signal), the peak-and-roll (peak signal), and the sustained recovery (early expansion signal) that a numbers table hides until it is obvious — and therefore too late.

Module 08 · Skill Building

Phase Transition Detection — Where the Edge Lives

Detecting transitions before they become obvious is the highest-value skill in Layer 1. The crowd enters when coincident data confirms the transition. You enter when leading indicators signal it. That time advantage is the difference between a great entry and an average one.

Peak Detection — 5 Signals

Signal 1: PMI declining for 3+ consecutive months from its peak, still above 50.

Signal 2: Yield curve flat to slightly inverted (−0.2% to +0.2% range).

Signal 3: Consumer confidence falling from its all-time high even if still elevated in absolute terms.

Signal 4: Unemployment rate first tick up from its record low — even 0.1% matters (3.4%→3.5%).

Signal 5: CB on hold for 2+ consecutive meetings after active hiking. FedWatch/OIS begins pricing future cuts.

Action: 3 of 5 signals present = begin reducing longs, initiate short positions. 4 or 5 = maximum short conviction.

Trough Detection — 5 Signals

Signal 1: GDP losses decelerating sharply (−1.4%, −0.9%, −0.2%) — the bleeding is stopping.

Signal 2: PMI rising for 3+ consecutive months from its bottom, still below 50 but approaching it.

Signal 3: Credit spreads tightening from extreme wides (1,100bp→840bp→620bp).

Signal 4: Building permits first positive monthly print in 6+ months.

Signal 5: Consumer confidence stabilising for 2+ months after a sustained sharp decline.

Action: 3 of 5 signals = cover shorts, begin building longs. Do NOT wait for unemployment to fall. It lags by 3–6 months. Enter early.

The False Dawn Warning — Most Costly Transition Error

A genuine recovery is self-sustaining and private-sector led. A false dawn is policy-stimulus-driven. The diagnostic check: is private sector credit growing? If GDP turns positive but private credit is still contracting (−3% YoY) despite rates at the floor, the GDP bounce is government spending — it will reverse when the stimulus fades. Require: PMI above 50 for 2+ months AND private credit turning positive AND credit spreads normalising before declaring genuine recovery and running full-size longs.

The Behind-the-Curve Signal — The Most Powerful Macro Setup

When leading indicators are screaming overheating (PMI above 60, M2 up 20%, CPI rising sharply) but the CB is still on hold using "transitory" language, you have the behind-the-curve setup. This creates the most violent eventual currency moves in macro trading. The CB will eventually be forced to hike far more aggressively than priced. When they do, the currency surges violently as markets reprice 0%→5% in 12 months. The 2022 USD surge from DXY 89 to 115 was exactly this mechanism. Identify it from leading data. Position before the forced hike cycle begins.

Module 09 · Skill Building

Multi-Economy Comparison Framework

No currency pair exists in isolation. EUR/USD is the differential between the US and Eurozone cycles. This is how you convert two individual cycle reads into one high-conviction trade decision.

01

Run the full cycle scan for each economy independently first

Before comparing anything, establish each economy's independent cycle phase and trajectory using the full leading indicator panel. Write it: Economy A verdict: [phase], [accelerating/decelerating], [CB next move]. Do the same for Economy B. Only then compare. The comparison is meaningless if either individual read is wrong.

02

Calculate the rate differential and its direction of travel

Real Rate = Nominal CB Rate minus Inflation. Calculate for both. Identify: (a) which has the higher real rate today, and (b) is the differential widening or narrowing? Plot TVC:US02Y-TVC:DE02Y for EUR/USD, TVC:US10Y-TVC:JP10Y for USD/JPY. The direction of travel of the spread is your medium-term currency signal — more important than the current level.

03

Find the maximum cycle phase divergence

The widest possible divergence = Mid Expansion vs. Contraction. One CB hiking while the other is cutting. This is always your highest-conviction bilateral trade. Never trade two economies both at Late Expansion — the divergence is thin, the noise large, the move small. Always ask: which pair has the widest phase gap this week? That is the pair to size up.

04

Define hold criteria and exit signal before entering

Write before you enter: "I will hold Long USD / Short EUR while: (a) US PMI stays above 52, (b) ECB is cutting or on hold, (c) rate differential is widening in USD's favour." Define the exit: "I exit if EZ PMI rises above 53 for 2 months, OR US PMI breaks below 50, OR the 2yr spread narrows below X%." Written criteria prevent emotional decision-making when individual prints go against you temporarily.

05

For commodity currencies — add the commodity cycle overlay

AUD/USD: Run Australia's domestic cycle AND China's Caixin PMI + iron ore price direction. China's cycle can override RBA's cycle entirely when the divergence is large. USD/CAD: domestic scan + WTI oil direction. NOK: domestic scan + Brent direction. When domestic cycle and commodity cycle both point the same direction, you have the highest-conviction commodity currency trade available.

Press Conference Reading Framework
  • Listen for changes from the previous meeting's language — that delta is the signal, not the absolute wording.
  • Which mandate is mentioned first? Inflation before growth = hawkish. Growth before inflation = pivoting dovish.
  • Count uncertainty words ("may," "if warranted," "should") vs commitment words ("will," "are committed," "determined"). More uncertainty = closer to neutral.
  • The Q&A is often more revealing than the prepared statement — journalists ask what the CB didn't want to address.
Module 11 · The Weekly Process

The Sunday Cycle Scan — Your Weekly Ritual

Every Sunday before markets open, you run this scan. 45–90 minutes until mastered, 20–30 minutes after. The output is your directional bias for every pair you trade. This is non-negotiable discipline.

Mentor

Do not skip this because you think you know the story from last week. The market changes. Your edge is in running this systematically, updating your view with fresh data, and NOT letting last week's conviction anchor this week's read. The trader who stops running the scan eventually stops seeing the turns coming. This is not optional work. This is the work.

  • Step 1: Pull the economic calendar. Identify every high-impact release for each economy you trade this week. Mark date, time, prior reading, and consensus estimate. This tells you which sessions will have the biggest moves.
  • Step 2: Update your cycle phase verdict for each economy. Has any data from the past week shifted the read? Check the latest PMI, confidence, and any GDP or employment data released. Record: phase, trajectory (accelerating or decelerating), confidence level 1–10.
  • Step 3: Check the full leading indicator panel. PMI and sub-indices direction. Yield curve shape. Credit spread trend (FRED:BAMLH0A0HYM2). Consumer confidence. Building permits 3-month trend. Are 2+ leading indicators aligned in the same direction?
  • Step 4: Plot and assess key rate differentials. EUR/USD: TVC:US02Y−TVC:DE02Y. USD/JPY: TVC:US10Y−TVC:JP10Y. GBP/USD: TVC:GB02Y−TVC:US02Y. Is the differential widening or narrowing vs. last week? The direction of travel of the spread is your medium-term currency signal.
  • Step 5: Decode the latest CB communications. Any speeches, minutes, decisions, or statements from the past week? Apply the language decoder. Has the language shifted from the prior reading? Even a subtle shift from "committed" to "monitoring carefully" is a meaningful dovish signal to price in.
  • Step 6: Check the risk sentiment dashboard. VIX level and direction. AUD/JPY. HY credit spreads. Is the environment open to expressing your structural thesis? If VIX above 25 and rising, size down regardless of how clean the cycle story looks.
  • Step 7: Write your weekly verdict for each pair. Format: "[Economy A] is in [phase], trajectory [accelerating/stable/decelerating]. [Economy B] is in [phase]. Rate differential is [widening/narrowing] in [A]'s favour. Bias: [Long/Short/Neutral]. Hold while [criteria]. Exit if [criteria]."
  • Step 8: Set event-based response plans. For each major release this week, define in advance: "If this prints above consensus, it [confirms/contradicts] my thesis. Action: [add/reduce/exit]." Pre-thought responses prevent panic reactions when prints surprise.
The Weekly Bias Rule

Trade WITH your structural bias at full size. Trade AGAINST it at reduced size (30–50%), only with tighter stops, and only when the counter-trade has a specific short-term catalyst that doesn't change the underlying thesis. If you are short EUR/USD on cycle divergence and a one-off EZ PMI beat sends it 80 pips higher, that is a retracement to sell into — not a signal to flip your thesis.
